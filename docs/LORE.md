# UNSTRUNG — Lore Bible

> *"When the Moon's necklace snapped, her beads fell into the dark and each became a kingdom.
> One bead fell last, and smallest, and rolled."*

This is the canonical story of UNSTRUNG. Text in game (`src/data/lore.js`, `src/data/npcs.js`,
`src/data/story.js`) should agree with it. The player is never told all of this directly: it arrives
in fragments, and some of it is only ever implied.

---

## 1. The shape of the world

**The Dark.** Before anything, there was a deep, soft dark with no bottom. Nothing in it shone.

**The Weaver.** Something lived in the dark that could make thread from itself: the Weaver, a vast,
patient spinner with many careful hands. She had no light, so she worked by touch. Her thread is red,
because it is spun from the inside of her.

**Selene, the Moon.** One night a light came into the dark: Selene. She was beautiful and she was
*leaking*. Her light ran off her like water off a stone and fell away into the dark, and was lost, so
she was always a little thinner than the night before. She would have run out.

**The Strand.** The Weaver loved her the way a maker loves a thing that is coming apart. She spun a
single red thread and caught the Moon's falling light on it, drop by drop, and each drop hardened into
a bead. Six drops were very large: the **six Great Beads**. A thousand were small: the **lesser
beads**. And the very last drop, the smallest, she shaped into a **clasp**, so the two ends of the
strand could meet behind the Moon's neck. Wearing the Strand, Selene stopped spilling. She was whole.
She was bright. She was, the Weaver thought, safe.

**The First Bead.** Before the Strand, the Weaver made one bead alone, to see if it could be done.
It came out too bright and too round and it would not stay on any thread: it rolled off every time.
She could not bring herself to unmake it, so she set it aside in a golden hall at the edge of the
world where the only sound is your own voice coming back: the **Hall of Echoes**. It has been waiting
there since before the Moon, listening to itself.

**The Hollow Thread.** Every thread has a hollow at its middle; that is what lets it bend. The
Strand's hollow was very long and very dark and, in time, it learned to *want*. It wanted to hold.
It pulled the strand tighter, year after year, until the beads ground against each other and the
Moon's neck bowed. It is not a creature. It is the keeping itself: the part of love that would rather
hold a thing than let it shine. Players meet it only in the true ending.

## 2. The Unstringing

Every song in the kingdoms says the Strand *snapped*. It did not.

The clasp was the only bead that touched both ends of the Strand, so it was the only one that could
feel all of it at once: every bead straining against the cord toward the dark below, where they had
never been; the Moon's head bent low under the weight; the Hollow Thread drawing tighter. One night
the clasp **opened**. It let go.

The beads poured off the Moon and fell, and the clasp fell after them, last and smallest. It struck
the dark at the strand's frayed end (**the Fray**), rolled, and forgot everything. It woke with two
big black eyes and a curl of red thread: the last scrap of the Strand, still knotted through it.

**That is Bead.** Bead is the clasp. Bead does not know this until the Cradle.

## 3. The kingdoms

A bead's light, falling into the dark, does not go out. It **softens**. It becomes ground and air and
warmth. Things grow in it; creatures wake in it. Every kingdom below is the light of a fallen bead,
and every creature in it is, in some distant way, made of the Moon.

- **Lesser beads** made the small places: the moss and bells of **Mossbell Hollows**, the glass
  greenhouse of **Glasswing Gardens**, the wax crypts of **Tallow Catacombs**, the sleeping
  **Hushwood**, the **Brine Cistern**, the clockwork **Cogwork Bastion**.
- **The six Great Beads** fell deepest and burned hottest, and each became the heart of a great
  kingdom. Each was claimed by a **Keeper** who loved it, and each Keeper's love went wrong in its own
  way.
- **Beadlings** are chips and seed-pearls knocked off the Strand in the fall. Collectors bottle them
  in jars because they glow. There are forty-eight.
- **Spindletown** was built on the surface around the **Old Spire** by creatures who climbed up out of
  the dark to be nearer the Moon. It rains there constantly. The Moon looks down through the clouds,
  smaller and paler every year.

### The six Keepers

| Great Bead | Kingdom | Keeper | How their love went wrong |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Ashen Kilns | **Vitrius**, the Glassblower | Tried to *copy* the bead: blew a thousand glass replicas to light every kiln. None of them shine. He keeps the real one in his own molten chest. |
| 2 | Mirrored Deep | **Prism Warden**, Keeper of the Many Lights | Split the bead's light into a million slivers so everyone could have some. Now the Deep is all reflections and no one can tell which light is real. |
| 3 | Bellfry Heights | **Carillon**, the Great Bell | Hung the bead inside himself as a clapper and rang it every hour so the Moon would know where it was. Rang until he cracked. |
| 4 | Silken Archive | **Archivist Quill**, Who Writes Every Ending | Read the bead's light like a page and began to write down everything, including what has not happened yet. He has already written Bead's ending. He does not show anyone which one. |
| 5 | Rotgut Warrens | **Broodmother Gnash** | Swallowed the bead to protect her brood from thieves. It glows in her belly. Her hunger grew around it, and the Warrens grew around her hunger. |
| 6 | Frostlit Reliquary | **Rimehorn**, the Frozen Stag | Froze the bead so it would never change, never dim, never leave. Pilgrims came to keep relics cold forever. Everything there is preserved and nothing is alive. |

The Keepers are not villains. Each one is holding on. Each one, defeated, lets go.

## 4. After the fall

**Selene** without her Strand began spilling again. She has been thinning for an age, coming apart at
her edges into silver threads. She waits at the **Moon's Cradle**, the high place where the Strand
once hung, calling her beads home. She no longer quite remembers why she needs them. She only knows
she is cold. When Bead arrives carrying the six Great Beads, she cannot help herself: she reaches.

**The Weaver** went down to the very bottom of the dark, to the **Loom Below**, and has been weaving
ever since: a new strand, longer and stronger, one that will never open. She has woven most of the
deep world in the attempt; the threads of every kingdom run back to her. She keeps the **Moon Key**,
which opens the gate above Spindletown to the Cradle. She fights Bead because she cannot hand the key
to anyone who has not proved they can carry what it opens. Defeated, she recognizes the clasp.
She says only: *"Finish it."*

**The Unwoven** lies past the Loom's frayed edge, where threads come undone. **The Unraveller**, the
end of every thread, keeps the **Void Thread** there: a thread that is not there. Nothing can hold
on to nothing, so the Void Thread can cut anything.

**The Fray** is the strand's frayed end, where it landed. **Gnarlknot**, the Tangled Warden, is the
knot that formed there in the fall: the strand's last attempt to hold itself together.

## 5. The three endings

**A — "Restrung."** Bead defeats Selene with all six Great Beads. The beads rise out of the kingdoms
and thread themselves back onto the Strand; the kingdoms grow a little dimmer and a little quieter.
The Moon is whole. Someone must close the clasp. Bead does. Bead is gone from the world below, and
from Spindletown, if you look up on a clear night (there are none), there is a small bright point at
the Moon's throat. *A sacrifice. The world is saved by being held.*

**B — "Unstrung."** Bead carries the Void Thread into the Cradle. When Selene falls, the Hollow Thread
rises out of the Strand: the wanting itself. Bead defeats it, and then does what it did once before,
on purpose this time: it bites through the red thread, its own curl, the last of the Strand. Every bead
is free. The kingdoms keep their lights. Selene does not need holding: her light falls on the kingdoms
below and is not lost, because it never was. It fell to make the world. The Moon rises over the
kingdoms as herself, bare and small and bright. Bead rolls home with no thread at all. *The true ending.*

**C — "First Light."** After Bead has answered every Echo in the Hall, the First Bead (*one was never
asked*) asks to be answered. It is the bead that would not stay on a thread; Bead is the bead that let
go. They fight the way two bells ring. When it ends they touch, the first and the last, and the light
between them is not moonlight. It is the first morning the deep world has ever had. *"Before the
strand, there was a single bead. Now there are two."*

## 6. Characters

### Spindletown
- **Mother Bobbin** (`bobbin`): a stout spool-woman, shopkeeper, gossip, unofficial mother of the
  town. Thread-wound body, needles in her bun. Sells the Glowwick Lantern, Knots, fragments and
  notches. Grows fond of Bead; worries out loud. After Ending A she keeps Bead's shelf "just in case".
- **Tinsmith Ferrule** (`tinsmith`): a gruff old dung beetle smith with a tin apron. Polishes Bead's
  shell with Pale Tin (attack upgrades). Speaks in short sentences. Lost his casting mold in the dark;
  bring the Tinsmith's Mold back and he pays well and nearly smiles.
- **The Beadmother** (`beadmother`): an old, round, shawled woman who gathers rescued Beadlings and
  wears them on a cord. She is a **knot**: one of the small knots tied between beads on the Strand so
  they would not wear each other away. She fell with the little ones and has looked after them since.
  At forty-eight she tells Bead who she is, and that she knew what Bead was the moment it rolled in.
- **Gossamer** (`gossamer`): a dusty, dignified moth who runs the Moth Ferries. Sells the Ferry Pass.
  Talks about flight the way old sailors talk about the sea. Moths fly toward light; Gossamer has
  spent her whole life trying not to fly at the Moon.
- **Elder Loam** (`elder`): an ancient mossy snail, the oldest creature in town. Was alive on the
  night of the fall and saw the beads come down "like slow rain that glowed". Guards the Moongate.
  Gives Bead the **Echo Bell** after an ending.
- **The Rollmaster Trio**: three ball-shaped martial artists, rivals and old friends.
  **Master Whirl** (`rollmaster_spin`, the dojo, Spin), **Master Carom** (`rollmaster_rebound`,
  Hushwood, Rebound), **Master Vortice** (`rollmaster_cyclone`, Bellfry, Cyclone). Each teaches a
  technique for glints. When Bead has all three, the masters gather in the dojo for a final test.

### The Fray
- **Threadwisp** (`threadwisp`): a tiny guide spirit, a loose fibre of the Strand that kept a little
  light. Greets Bead in the first room, teaches Bonk and Mend, and says more than it means to.

### The road
- **Sir Burl of the Curled Order** (`burl_hollows` → `burl_kilns` → `burl_loom`): a pill-bug knight
  armed with a pin. He curls into a ball when frightened, and so he is certain he is the Moon's lost
  bead and is rolling home to her. Funny in the Hollows (stuck below a ledge he cannot climb);
  scorched in the Kilns (trying to bake his shell into glass so she will recognise him); found at the
  very bottom of the Loom ("I rolled downhill. It is the only direction I know"), where he understands
  he is only a bug who was frightened for a long time. He gives Bead his fortune (37 Glints), asks it
  to tell the Moon a knight tried, and curls up for the last time. After that he is a still, grey
  ball, and looks, at last, very much like a bead.
- **Bellkeeper Tull** (`bellkeeper`, Hollows): a deaf old beetle who still rings the Hollows' bells by
  feel, so the Mossbug's children sleep. Hints at the Brass Beetle.
- **Pell the Mothmaid** (`mothmaid`, Gardens): a glasswing gardener tending a greenhouse with no sun.
  Her sisters became the Twin Glassmoths, Lumen and Lacuna.
- **The Chandler** (`chandler`, Catacombs): a melting candle who keeps the crypt lights. Warns Bead
  about the dark below and the Lantern.
- **Hushling** (`hushling`, Hushwood): a small mushroom who only whispers, so as not to wake the
  Elder. Afraid of the owl.
- **Tinkerbot Nine** (`tinkerbot`, Bastion): a tiny automaton of the vanished Guild, still awaiting
  orders. Bead gives it some, eventually.
- **Old Brine** (`diver`, Cistern): a hermit crab in a diving helmet, the last Pumpkeeper.
- **Brother Cullet** (`glassmonk`, Kilns): a monk of the Blown Flame, Vitrius's last disciple, who
  believes glass is light that agreed to stay.
- **The Twinseer** (`twinseer`, Mirror): a seer with two faces, one that tells the truth and one that
  is a reflection of the other. They disagree about which is which.
- **Ringer Wren** (`ringer`, Bellfry): a small, tough wren who climbs the towers to tie down loose
  bells against the wind.
- **The Librarian** (`librarian`, Archive): a bookmoth who has eaten so many books she is mostly
  footnotes. Shushes Bead. Fears Quill has written her ending too.
- **Vesp** (`hivedeserter`, Warrens): a wasp who would not sting, and fled the Stinger Court.
- **The Frozen Pilgrim** (`frozenpilgrim`, Frost): a pilgrim who carried a little warmth up the Long
  Cold to give to the relics, and stopped to rest. He is very slow now. When Rimehorn falls, he thaws
  enough to finish his walk, and is gone.
- **Echo of the Weaver** (`weaverecho`, Loom): a memory that broke off the Weaver long ago and still
  works a phantom loom. Calm, sad, and frank. Tells Bead what it is, gently, before the Weaver does not.
- **The Moonchild** (`moonchild`, Cradle): the last little light Selene kept, a child of moonlight on
  the final balcony. Tells Bead it is the clasp. Hints that something that is not there could cut
  what holds her.
- **Motley** (`lintjester`, Dunes): jester to the Lint King. Formerly one of two jesters. Puns.
- **The Echokeeper** (`echokeeper`, Echoes): a hollow bell-shaped attendant who keeps the Hall and its
  four Pantheons, and knows about the one who was never asked.
